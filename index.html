Fertility Rate in the World

Fertility rate is the average number of children a woman is expected to have during her lifetime. Around the world, fertility rates have been falling due to education, urbanization, healthcare, and economic change.

What Is Fertility Rate?

Fertility rate measures how many children women have on average. A rate of about 2.1 children per woman is called the replacement level. This is the level needed to keep a population stable without migration.

Global Trend

Many countries now have fertility rates below replacement level. This means their populations may age and eventually shrink. Other countries, especially in parts of Africa, still have higher fertility rates and younger populations.

Why Fertility Rates Are Falling

Education

More years in school often lead people to have children later in life.

Urbanization

City life is usually more expensive, making large families less common.

Healthcare

Better healthcare and family planning give families more control.

Economy

Housing, jobs, and childcare costs influence family size decisions.

Why It Matters

Fertility rates affect schools, jobs, healthcare systems, pensions, and the future size of populations. Understanding fertility trends helps governments and communities plan for the future.
