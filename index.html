Overview

A world below replacement level

The replacement fertility rate — the level needed to maintain a stable population without migration — is approximately 2.1 children per woman. All three nations covered here have fallen at or below this threshold, signalling a profound shift in global demographics with far-reaching economic and social consequences.

🇧🇷
Brazil
From 6.2 to 1.6 in sixty years

Brazil achieved one of Latin America's fastest demographic transitions, driven by urbanisation, women entering the workforce, and broad access to family planning across its vast territory.

Historical Context

Key moments in demographic change

The decline in fertility rates across Brazil, India, and China was not gradual — it was shaped by deliberate policy decisions, economic shocks, and cultural transformations that unfolded over decades.

1960s
Peak fertility across all three nations

Brazil, India, and China all recorded fertility rates above 5.5 children per woman during this decade. Post-war population growth was seen as a sign of national vitality in many developing economies.

1975 — Brazil
Urbanisation accelerates the transition

Brazil's rapid urbanisation through the 1970s fundamentally changed family structures. City living reduced the economic value of large families, and access to contraception expanded significantly.

1980 — China
The One-Child Policy is enacted

China's government introduced strict birth limits as part of its modernisation programme. Families were restricted to one child in urban areas, enforced through fines, loss of benefits, and in some cases coercion.

1990s — India
Economic liberalisation changes the landscape

India's 1991 economic reforms opened the country to global markets, accelerating urbanisation and dramatically improving female literacy rates — two of the strongest predictors of fertility decline.

2015 — China
The One-Child Policy is abolished

Facing a rapidly ageing population and shrinking workforce, China abolished the One-Child Policy in 2015 and later introduced a three-child policy in 2021. Fertility rates have not recovered.

2023 — All Three
All three nations below replacement level

For the first time in recorded history, Brazil, India, and China simultaneously record fertility rates at or below the replacement threshold of 2.1 — a landmark moment in global demographic history.
